# AXIOM 2.0 — American English Standard

Status: normative engineering contract. Defines what REDLINE considers
"correct" English and how correctness separates from usage, register, style,
voice, and rewriting.

## 1. Dialect

**Contemporary Standard American English / en-US.**

Runtime/default recommendations are calibrated to American English. Research
resources from outside the U.S. may inform general linguistic concepts, but
they do not set REDLINE's normative default.

### American conventions (default)

- `color`, `organization`, `analyze`
- not `colour`, `organisation`, `analyse`

### Do not blindly "correct"

Quoted British text, proper names, titles, source material, or intentional
dialect must not be flagged solely for British conventions.

## 2. The three (four) language questions

AXIOM 2.0 distinguishes:

1. **Grammatical truth** — Is it valid contemporary Standard American English?
2. **Usage truth** — Do competent American speakers/writers actually use this
   construction?
3. **Register appropriateness** — Is it appropriate for this audience/purpose?

My Voice adds a fourth:

4. **Personal preference** — Among valid options, which sounds like the owner?

These must never be conflated.

## 3. Correctness dimensions

Objective correctness covers:

- grammar
- spelling
- morphology
- syntax
- punctuation

Usage is not correctness: legitimate contemporary American usage does not
become an error solely because it is conversational.

## 4. Writing Context contract

Writing Context = **General / Professional / Academic**. These are writing
contexts, not profiles, personas, or separate grammar engines.

Grammar correctness is identical across all three. Example:
`The students is studying.` is incorrect in General, Professional, and
Academic.

Writing Context changes **advisory (Tier 4) behavior**, not grammatical truth.

### General

Contemporary Standard American English with minimal unnecessary stylistic
intervention. Tolerates legitimate contractions, first person, conversational
constructions, reasonable fragments/ellipsis where context supports them,
phrasal verbs, ordinary vocabulary, intentional repetition, natural cadence.
"General" does not mean "formal."

### Professional

Same grammar rules; increased advisory sensitivity to clarity, concision,
reader purpose, actionability, unnecessary jargon, unexplained acronyms,
buried key points, excessive qualifiers, overly complex sentences, and
inconsistent terminology.

Professional does NOT mean: eliminate contractions, use bigger words, sound
corporate or legalistic, or remove legitimate technical vocabulary. It means
**clear + concise + purposeful + audience-aware**.

"Jargon" means language that obscures communication for the intended
audience, not all technical vocabulary.

### Academic

Same grammar rules; increased advisory sensitivity to precision of claims,
logical progression, ambiguity, terminology consistency,
discipline-appropriate register, overly conversational phrasing, sentence
clarity, organization, and unsupported absolute wording.

Academic must NOT automatically prohibit contractions, first person, passive
voice, longer sentences, or technical terminology. Those depend on
discipline, audience, assignment, and chosen style guide.

APA/MLA/Chicago rules are future optional Style Guide layers, not universal
grammar rules.

## 5. Register

Register is advisory. Spoken-American corpora are usage evidence, not written
grammar authority. General tolerates conversational written structures where
appropriate; Professional and Academic may advise differently.

## 6. Style

Disputed conventions become preferences/refinements unless objectively
necessary for clarity. When reputable American authorities disagree, default
to **Style / Preference**, not **Grammar Error**.

Examples of legitimate disputes:

- serial comma
- contractions
- first-person use
- some capitalization conventions
- passive voice preferences

A future Style Guide layer may enforce APA, MLA, Chicago, AP, or custom
personal/project rules, independent of AXIOM correctness.

### Serial comma policy test

`apples, oranges and bananas` is not automatically a grammar error merely
because one style guide prefers `apples, oranges, and bananas`. Ambiguity may
attract clarity advice; a chosen Style Guide may add a style-layer
preference. Base AXIOM stays neutral.

## 7. Voice

Valid personal choices remain valid. My Voice must not redefine grammar. Its
lanes are exactly **Natural / Professional / Academic** (no separate Casual
lane).

- Natural Voice preserves contractions, directness, opinions, skepticism,
  natural emphasis, intentional repetition, normal vocabulary, personal
  cadence; it cleans filler, accidental duplication, abandoned starts,
  transcription artifacts, unclear ordering.
- Professional/Academic Voice retain the owner's recognizable voice while
  increasing clarity, precision, structure, and appropriateness.

Never manufacture facts, citations, evidence, or claims.

## 8. Transform

Substantial rewriting requires explicit user action. Transform is separate
from Writing Context and My Voice:

- Writing Context = "judge optional refinements for this kind of writing."
- My Voice = "if helping me rewrite, preserve my voice while making it
  appropriate."
- Transform = "explicitly rewrite this selection."

Do not collapse these concepts.

## 9. Natural American English preservation

Create explicit negative controls where conversational American writing is
valid: contractions, conversational syntax, ellipsis, fragments where context
makes them legitimate, discourse markers, colloquial-but-standard forms,
phrasal verbs, repetition for emphasis. A contextual model that boosts recall
by destroying natural writing is not acceptable.

## 10. Enforceability

This standard is the acceptance criterion for every rule, dataset, and model
recommendation in the AXIOM 2.0 research. Any finding that treats a valid
American usage as an error, or a style dispute as a grammar error, is a
defect regardless of its source.
